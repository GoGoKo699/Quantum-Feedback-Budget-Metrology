# Related work

## Monitored sensing and feedback

Albarelli, Rossi, Tamascelli and Genoni treat the same independent transverse Lindblad noise and a GHZ probe. Their frequency-information formula in Eq. (19) agrees with this project's zero-action expression after setting their rate $`\kappa = 2\gamma`$. They compute the system–environment benchmark analytically and report numerical saturation under perfect photodetection up to fourteen sensors. The [compensated-count proof](../science/fisher-bound.md) recovers their expression from the integrated signs and proves its optimality among all initial probes under the zero-action rule. The positive hard-cap theorem instead optimizes over physical control in the interrogation, and its fixed-array long-time limit answers a different scaling question from growth in sensor count at fixed time. [Albarelli et al., Quantum 2, 110 (2018), Sec. 3.2](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2018-12-03-110)

Ma, Pang, Chen and Yang develop a trajectory formulation of estimation that adds the information of the classical record to the mean conditional-state information. In this model the first term vanishes because the marks are exogenous. The trajectory formulation supplies the starting point for the proof. The bound on changing signal signs and the optimization over available action follow from the marked dynamics and hard cap. [Ma et al., Phys. Rev. A 99, 032347 (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.99.032347)

The educational anchor derives continuous photodetection and orders jump-triggered feedback after the detected jump. With jump operator $`X_i`$ the count intensity is constant. Correcting every marked $`X_i`$ immediately is the familiar canceling-feedback limit. The protocol schedules and caps the corrections, and its converse applies with the same action constraint even to controls that change the Heisenberg observables coherently. [Albarelli and Genoni, Phys. Lett. A 494, 129260 (2024), Secs. III–IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.13214v1)

Xu and coauthors use learned closed-loop trajectory feedback to recover signal sensitivity in quantum sensing. This provides a computational control approach for richer monitored dynamics. Here the exact unitary-jump interface yields a closed-form allocation and a matching bound under the hard action cap. [Xu et al., arXiv:2508.11198v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11198v4)

## Syndrome recovery, timing and finite budgets

Kessler, Lovchinsky, Sushkov and Lukin use a two-qubit repetition code and periodic error recovery for a transverse sensing problem, written in rotated Pauli axes. A syndrome report there does not reveal the time of a jump inside the interval. The [coherent refresh example](../science/latency.md#coherent-refresh-can-do-better) uses this established correction mechanism and makes a different comparison: the controller pays for each refresh, and the full marked record eventually arrives for the phase readout. That model allows a strict comparison of long-time coefficients under the same action charge. [Kessler et al., Phys. Rev. Lett. 112, 150802 (2014), Fig. 1](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.112.150802)

Shettell, Munro, Markham and Nemoto quantify the effect of finite intervals between repetition-code recoveries. Here a hard total cap charges changes of individual $`Z_i`$ observables and the controller can respond to continuously marked jumps. The exact positive-delay result applies to controls preserving each $`Z_i`$ up to sign. A general coherent optimum at positive delay is still unresolved. Kwon and coauthors analyze a finite engineered dissipation rate in autonomous recovery, with a different noise condition and a different charged resource. [Shettell et al., New J. Phys. 23, 043038 (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/abf533) · [Kwon et al., npj Quantum Inf. 12, 103 (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41534-026-01268-1)

[Finite-Cadence Quantum Metrology](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Finite-Cadence-Quantum-Metrology) charges cells between boundary operations in an unmonitored model and studies global minimax precision. [Markovian Resolution Limit](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Markovian-Resolution-Limit) charges a number of contacts and hidden pulse jitter in an explicit collision model. This account uses marked bit flips, sensor-level action and a local information criterion. The three budgets have operationally different meanings.
